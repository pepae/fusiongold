Method
  1. Fusion grows on an S-curve from 1 GW in 2031 to your 2050 level, then 3% a year.
  2. Gold output = gold-making GW × blanket yield ÷ 40% efficiency × 80% uptime.
  3. Blankets lock up mercury, and each tonne of gold uses up about a tonne of convertible mercury. When supply runs out, gold-making capacity is capped, then shrinks.
  4. Once fusion output passes 25 t/yr, investors start pricing in the extra gold expected over the next 10 years. The price falls with the bigger effective stock (222,600 t today) and with holders who leave as gold stops being scarce.
  5. Investor reaction sets how much future gold is priced in (20% to 100%) and how many holders can leave (5% to 55%).
  6. Fixed: 10 years of cooling before sale (barely matters, markets price the metal early); mines cut 0.3% of output per 1% price drop; no new gold-making capacity once the price nears $1,100/oz.
